Basement Waterproofing in Brookline, MA

Interior drains, a sump basin that works, discharge piping that carries water away. We do the plumbing side of keeping a basement dry and tell you plainly when the cause is the wall or the grade.

Call (617) 752-0573

Basement waterproofing in Brookline, MA starts with finding where the water enters and what controls it. Some wet basements are a plumbing problem, some are a foundation problem, and some are both. We handle the plumbing side.

That means interior drain lines leading to a sump, the sump basin and pump, and discharge piping. It does not mean repairing structural masonry or poured foundation walls, which is a foundation specialist's work.

First look

Where basement water comes from

Pointing at the wrong source wastes money. We start by sorting the cause.

  • ✓A plumbing leak. Wet near a supply line, a water heater or a drain. A meter that moves with everything off is the tell. See leak detection.
  • ✓Groundwater through the slab or the floor joint. Water that rises after rain at the wall-floor seam. A drain and sump can manage this.
  • ✓Water through a wall crack. Often a foundation or grading problem, not a plumbing one.
  • ✓Sewer or floor-drain backup. Dirty water from a drain after heavy rain. That is a sewer line question.
  • ✓Condensation. Cold pipes and humid air in summer. Dehumidifying and insulating help more than drains.

What we install

Interior drains, sump basin and discharge

An interior drain collects water at the wall-floor joint and carries it to a sump basin. A basin holds the water until the pump moves it out through a discharge pipe with a check valve above the pump.

The pump side is covered on our sump pump page, including battery backup. This page is about the system as a whole. Where the slab must be opened for a drain run, we tell you what we cut, what we pour back, and what the finished floor looks like.

Old basements

What Brookline's housing age means underground

Brookline's median home was built in 1938, and about half of units date to 1939 or earlier, per Census ACS data. Foundations from that era are often stone, brick or early concrete, with floors that were never designed to stay dry.

That is why we look at the wall before quoting an interior drain. A drain moves water that gets in, but it does not repair a wall that is crumbling or pushing inward. About 77 percent of Brookline units are in buildings with two or more units, so a shared basement may also need the owner's sign-off.

Your limits

When to call a foundation specialist instead

  • Horizontal or stair-step cracks. . These can signal wall movement.
  • A wall that bows inward. . That is structural and needs an engineer.
  • Leaks that follow rain through the wall itself. . A drain at the floor will not stop water entering above it.
  • Rotting framing or crumbling mortar. . Damage that needs repair before any plumbing.

If we find structural signs, we say so and stop. We would rather tell you than take a job a drain will not fix.

Rules

Discharge, permits and the Town

Brookline's sewer guidance says downspouts and sump pumps do not discharge to sewer drains. Your discharge goes outside, away from the foundation, and it must not feed the sanitary system.

Plumbing work in Brookline requires a licensed plumber to pull any required permit, since homeowners cannot pull plumbing permits there. If the basement has a floor drain connected to the sewer, a backwater valve may be worth considering. The Town's policy covers fixtures below the roadway, and it requires a licensed plumber and a Building Department permit.

Know before you call

What sets the price of basement waterproofing

A new sump alone and a full interior drain around a finished basement are very different jobs.

  • Length of drainMore linear feet of floor cut and pipe means more labor and concrete.
  • Floor and finishBare slab is simpler. Finished floors, framing and paneling must be removed and restored.
  • Sump and backupA single pump costs less than a pump with a battery backup and alarm.
  • Discharge routeA short run to a safe outlet is simpler than a long run across the lot.
  • Access and permitsTight stairs, low ceilings and required permits all add time.

Standard homeowner policies often exclude groundwater and seepage. Ask your insurer before you assume coverage.
