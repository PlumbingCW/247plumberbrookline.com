Urgent

When a faucet problem is an emergency

Most drips are slow and can wait for a daytime visit. These cannot: a handle that spins without shutting off flow, a supply line spraying under the sink, water running down a wall from a tub spout, or a shower that will not stop.

Shut the nearest stop first. Under a sink it is a small oval or round handle on each of two lines, hot and cold. For a tub or shower, there is usually no stop at the valve, so the shutoff is the unit main or the building main.

Keep towels away from outlets and move anything valuable out of the cabinet. Then tell dispatch what the fixture is doing and whether the water is controlled.

Symptoms

Faucet signs that point to a worn part

  • ✓A steady drip from the spout. A worn washer, seat or cartridge seal that no longer closes fully.
  • ✓Water at the base of the handle. A cracked O-ring or a cartridge that has loosened in its body.
  • ✓Hot and cold mixing when the faucet is off. A failed check inside a single-handle cartridge or a mixing valve.
  • ✓Handle feels gritty or stiff. Sediment or a worn cartridge. It tends to fail all at once.
  • ✓Weak flow from one faucet only. A clogged aerator or cartridge screen, not a pressure problem in the building.
  • ✓Shower temperature swings. A worn pressure-balancing cartridge in the wall valve.
Diagnosis

Cartridge, stem, ball or disc: matching the repair to the faucet

We identify the faucet by taking it apart. Pull the handle, remove the retaining clip or nut, and look at what actually moves. A compression faucet has a rubber washer on a stem, while newer single-handle models use a cartridge, ball or ceramic disc.

Repair is often cheaper than replacement, but not always. If the body is corroded or the maker no longer sells the cartridge, replacing the fixture is the honest answer.

Old stops

Supply stops in older Brookline buildings

The median Brookline home was built in 1938, per Census ACS data, and many bathrooms and kitchens in those buildings still have their original stops. Packing nuts dry out, stems seize, and a stop that is turned once in forty years can drip for good afterward.

A broken or seized stop is the usual reason a simple faucet call grows. We replace it with a new quarter-turn valve and a braided supply line so the next repair has a working shutoff. A burst or broken supply line, sprung from the stop, is also a burst pipe repair question if water has left the cabinet.

Faucet repair itself does not need a permit, per the state's plumbing consumer fact sheet.

Showers and tubs

Shower valves and tub faucets

A shower valve sits behind the wall. Dripping from the showerhead after you shut it off, or a handle that will not stay put, usually means the cartridge or the diverter has worn. The valve itself rarely needs replacing, but the trim and handle have to come off to reach it.

Where the old valve has no service stops, we shut the unit main to work. When tile blocks access, we tell you before cutting anything and price the opening and patch. If the tub also drains slowly, that is a separate drain cleaning problem.

Before we arrive

What to do while you wait

Close the nearest stop

Under a sink, turn both oval handles clockwise. Do not force a stop that crumbles or hisses.

Empty the cabinet

Pull out cleaners and wet items so we can reach the lines and see the wall.

Note the brand

A name stamped on the handle or spout helps us bring the right cartridge on the first trip.

Find the unit main

Know where it is. A frozen stop means we shut the whole unit to work.

Know before you call

What sets the price of a faucet repair

A cartridge swap and a new stop with a new fixture are priced differently. This is what moves the number.

  • Repair or replacementParts and a short visit cost less than a new faucet, supply lines and installation.
  • Condition of the stopsA seized stop adds a new valve and time before the faucet can be serviced.
  • Fixture type and accessA wall-mounted tub valve behind tile takes longer to reach than a deck-mounted kitchen faucet.
  • Parts availabilityAn older or imported model may need a special-order cartridge, which can mean a second visit.
  • TimingA late-night or weekend call uses the same dispatch, and any after-hours part of the price is stated before work begins.

A sudden spray that soaks a cabinet or ceiling may be covered by a homeowner's or renter's policy, while a slow drip over years usually is not. Your insurer decides.
