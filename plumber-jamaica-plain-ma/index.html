The neighborhood

From the Arboretum to Centre Street

Jamaica Plain stretches from the Arnold Arboretum at 125 Arborway, a 281-acre part of the Emerald Necklace, down to Forest Hills and across to Centre Street, South Street and Washington Street. Mature trees line many of those blocks, and roots are a standard suspect when a main drain slows down every few months. If a snake clears it and it returns, a camera look at the line tells you whether roots are the cause.

Jamaica Pond, about 60 acres and the largest and deepest freshwater body in Boston, sits a short walk from the Jamaicaway. Living next to it does not change what comes out of your tap. The Boston Water and Sewer Commission (BWSC) delivers water that comes from MWRA's reservoirs, so a brown or sputtering tap is a plumbing question, not a pond question.

The buildings

Triple-deckers, condos and shared stacks

The City of Boston's planning profile (2010 Census) counts 17,650 housing units in Jamaica Plain. About 52 percent are condo units, 14.4 percent of properties are three-families and roughly 62 percent of units are rented. The triple-decker, which spread across New England in the 1890s, is still the picture most people have of the neighborhood.

Stacked units share risers and waste stacks, so one failed fitting can wet three kitchens. We find which line is leaking and shut only that branch where a valve allows it. If the source is in a neighbor's wall, we say so plainly.

Water and sewer

Who owns what: BWSC, you and the town permit

In Jamaica Plain, BWSC runs the water and sewer mains in the street. If the street itself is flooding, as it did on Heath Street in January 2023 when a 36-inch main broke, call BWSC's emergency line at 617-989-7000 first. Pressure loss across a whole block usually starts with the main, not your pipes.

Before we arrive

What to do in a triple-decker or condo while you wait

A few steps protect your floors and speed the visit.

Find your shut-offs

Locate the fixture valve and the main in your unit or basement. Tell us if either one will not turn.

Warn the unit below

If water is coming through, knock on the door below and move belongings away from the wet wall. Keep outlets off in soaked rooms.

Clear the route

Move boxes from the basement stairs, the water heater and the cleanout. Tell us the floor and where to park.

Gas smell means leave

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or National Grid (1-800-233-5325) before calling us.
