Emergency Hydro Jetting in Brookline, MA

A cable punches a hole through a clog. A jetter washes the whole pipe wall. We run both, and we tell you honestly when jetting is the wrong tool.

Call (617) 752-0573

If your main line is backing up into a basement shower or floor drain, call our 24/7 dispatch and then stop all water use in the building. That means no laundry, no dishwasher, no flushing, and no one in the wet area near outlets or a furnace. Water keeps flowing from upstairs fixtures even when you are not looking, so shut those down too.

Hydro jetting pushes high-pressure water through a hose with a rotating nozzle, and it cuts grease, rust scale and fine roots off the inside of a pipe. It suits a main sewer line or a large branch that keeps clogging after cabling. It is a poor fit for pipe that has cracked, sagged or lost joints, and a camera tells us which case you have.

How it works

What a jetter does that a cable cannot

A drum cable makes a path through a clog. It leaves a film of grease and scale on the wall, and that film catches the next load of paper and food. A jetter nozzle has rear-facing ports that pull the hose forward and spray a ring of water at the wall, so debris is scoured off and flushed toward the sewer.

We feed the hose in through a cleanout or, if there is none, through a pulled toilet or a roof vent. Water and slurry can surge back at the opening, so we lay protection and control the flow. A camera before and after shows the wall and confirms the result.

Good candidates

Problems where jetting is the right call

These patterns point to a coating on the pipe wall, not a single plug.

  • ✓Grease that returns. A line serving a busy kitchen, or a shared stack with years of cooking fat, clogs again a month after cabling.
  • ✓Rust scale in old iron. Rough, flaking cast iron narrows the bore and snags paper. Jetting knocks the loose scale out, though it will not smooth the pipe.
  • ✓Fine root hairs. Roots that enter at a joint grow as a mat of small threads. A cutting head clears the thick part and the jet removes the mat.
  • ✓Clogs the cable cannot finish. The cable passes, the sink drains for a day, and the gurgle comes back.

Where we say no

When jetting is the wrong tool

Water under pressure does damage to a failed pipe. If the camera shows a crack, a collapsed section, a belly full of standing water or an open joint, jetting can wash soil into the line and make things worse. Some mid-century lines were made of bituminous fiber, which softens with age and does not tolerate a jet well.

In those cases we stop, show you the video and explain the repair route instead. We would rather lose the jetting job than hand you a flooded yard.

Main lines

Main lines, branch lines and what to ask for

Many owners ask whether to jet only the main or the whole house. A kitchen branch that carries years of grease may need its own pass, while a quiet upstairs bath line may not. We point out which branches the camera flags and price those separately.

On a lateral that connects to the public main, the pipe from your foundation to the street is yours. The Town handles blockages inside the public main. Our sewer line cleaning page spells out that split and the backwater valve policy.

Before we arrive

Main line backing up: what to do first

A few quick steps protect the building until the machine is hooked up.

Stop all water

Shut off laundry, dishwasher and showers. Ask neighbors in a shared building to do the same.

Find the cleanout

Look for a capped pipe near the basement floor or where the main leaves the foundation. Clear a few feet around it.

Keep power dry

If water reaches an outlet, appliance or the furnace, stay clear and tell dispatch when you call.

Think about the pipe

Tell us roughly how old the building is and if you have had roots or repeated clogs before.

We offer same-day appointments when available, and dispatch is open overnight for main-line backups.

Know before you call

What drives the cost of hydro jetting

You hear a clear price before work starts. These factors move it.

  • Length and diameterA 4 inch line to the street takes longer than a short 3 inch branch.
  • Camera findingsA video run before and after is part of a proper jetting job, and the pipe condition decides whether jetting is safe.
  • Access pointA cleanout makes it simple. No cleanout means pulling a toilet or working through the roof.
  • Buildup typeSoft grease clears in a pass. Hard scale or roots may need cutting first and a second jetting pass.
  • Hour of the callAfter-hours dispatch is available, and timing can affect the price.

Many homeowner policies exclude sewer backup unless an endorsement was added. If you plan to file, ask us for a written description of the findings.
