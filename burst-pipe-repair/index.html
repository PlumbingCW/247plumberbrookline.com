Burst pipe repair in Brookline mostly means two situations: a supply line that split from freezing in a cold snap, and an old pipe that finally gave out at a joint or a corroded wall. Either way, the water keeps running until the valve is closed, so the sequence is stop the flow, drain the lines, find the break, repair, and test.

Right now

When a burst pipe is an emergency: what to do while you wait

Any pipe that is spraying, pouring or soaking a ceiling is an emergency. A slow weep at a joint can wait for a daytime visit, but we would still rather you call and let dispatch decide.

Close the main

In most Brookline buildings the main shut-off is in the basement near the meter. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

Open a low faucet

Open the lowest cold tap in the building to drain pressure and let remaining water leave the broken line.

Protect the room

Move rugs and boxes off the floor and put buckets under ceiling drips. Leave a sagging, water-heavy ceiling alone and keep people out from under it.

Stay clear of electricity

Do not touch outlets, lamps or appliances in wet areas. If the panel is in a flooded basement, call us and the electric utility rather than wade in.

If you cannot find the main valve, or it will not turn, call Brookline's Water and Sewer emergency line at 617-730-2175 and tell dispatch here. The Town's sidewalk shut-off is a last resort and we will tell you whether it applies.

Causes

Why pipes burst in older Brookline buildings

Per the Census Bureau's 2020-2024 American Community Survey, about 15.7 percent of Brookline housing units sit in 3 to 4 unit buildings, the triple-decker class, and 51.7 percent of all units were built in 1939 or earlier. Those buildings share a few weak points.

  • ✓Exterior walls. Brick walls hold cold, and a supply line on the outside wall of a bathroom or kitchen freezes first.
  • ✓Unheated spaces. Pipes in cellars, back porches, attics and rear stairwells lose heat fast.
  • ✓Old fittings. Galvanized threaded joints rust from the inside, thin the wall, and split under normal pressure.
  • ✓Vacant units. A unit left cold over a weekend can freeze a line that nobody is there to see.
  • ✓Pressure shocks. A hammering valve or an old shut-off can fatigue a pipe until it cracks.
The fix

How we repair a burst or frozen pipe

  1. Stop and drain. We confirm the main is closed, drain the damaged branch, and photograph the break so you have a record for your insurer.
  2. Find the whole damage. A frozen pipe often splits in more than one place. We pressure-test the nearby runs before we call it fixed.
  3. Cut out the bad section. We replace the damaged length with new pipe and fittings rather than patching a cracked wall.
  4. Thaw safely if needed. A frozen line that has not yet burst is thawed slowly with controlled heat. We never use an open flame.
  5. Test and restore. We bring the pressure back slowly, check every joint, and show you where the repair sits.

Where the pipe is inside a finished wall, we cut a small access panel and leave you with a clean opening for drywall and paint. That finish work is a separate trade.

Prevent the next one

Freezing in older brick buildings: prevention that works

Open the cabinet doors under a sink on an outside wall during hard cold. Let a faucet drip on the coldest nights, and keep heat on in vacant units. Wrapping exposed cellar pipe helps, and moving a vulnerable line deeper into the building is the permanent fix.

CBS Boston reported in January 2014 that a nor'easter followed by frigid temperatures caused broken pipes throughout the city. The lesson is that freezing is a pattern here, not a rarity, and the prep is cheap compared with a flooded unit.

Know before you call

What sets the cost of a burst pipe repair

A burst pipe is priced after we see it, and we give you the clear price before work starts. These are the factors.

  • Pipe materialCopper, galvanized, PEX and old lead-solder joints each need different parts and methods.
  • Location of the breakA cellar ceiling is quick. A line buried in a plaster wall or a masonry chase takes longer.
  • Number of breaksFrozen lines often fail in several places, and every extra repair adds time.
  • Water damage nearbyIf surrounding pipe is corroded, we may recommend replacing a longer run now.
  • Time of the callA midnight burst needs a different response from a morning visit for a slow weep.

Burst pipes are usually the covered case under a homeowner policy, because the damage is sudden. Gradual leaks and wear often are not, and condo and renter policies split things by who owns the pipe. Read your policy, photograph everything, and keep our invoice.
