Cleaning Drains with Vinegar and Baking Soda in Brookline, MA

A fizzing mix is fine for a smelly, slow drain. It will not clear a real clog, and it is dangerous next to commercial cleaners.

By the Brookline 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 23, 2026 · Updated October 23, 2026

Call (617) 752-0573

Vinegar and baking soda can freshen a sink drain and loosen a thin film of soap and grease. They will not dissolve hair, a wad of wipes, tree roots or a solid blockage. If a drain is slow but still moving, the mix is a safe first try. If water is standing or backing up, skip it and call.

The fizz you see is carbon dioxide gas, plus water and a mild salt called sodium acetate. It is gentle on pipes, which is both its strength and its limit. Below you will find the method, the safe limits, the one mistake that can hurt you, and when a plumber needs to take over.

The method

How to use it on a slow kitchen or bathroom drain

Use this on a drain that still carries water, not one that is full.

  1. Clear the surface. Remove the stopper or strainer and pull out any hair or food you can reach.
  2. Pour hot tap water. Run about a quart of hot water down the drain. Use hot from the tap, not boiling, if you have PVC traps or a cracked porcelain sink.
  3. Add the baking soda. Pour in half a cup and let it settle into the pipe.
  4. Add the vinegar. Pour in half a cup of white vinegar, then plug the opening so the fizz works down, not up.
  5. Wait fifteen minutes. Then flush with more hot tap water for a minute or two.
  6. Repeat once if needed. If the drain is not clearly faster after two tries, stop.

Safety

Never mix it with commercial drain cleaners

Do not pour vinegar into a drain that has had bleach, lye or an acid cleaner in it. Vinegar and bleach release chlorine gas. Vinegar added to a lye cleaner reacts hard and can spatter. If a cleaner already failed, leave the drain alone and tell the plumber exactly what went in.

The cleaner is also a hazard for the person who comes after you. Standing water in a sink that holds lye or acid can burn skin and eyes. That is why we ask what has been poured before we put a cable in the line.

Chemical cleaner risk

Why harsh drain cleaners are a bad idea in older pipes

Commercial cleaners work by heat or strong chemistry. In old buildings the pipe is part of the problem.

  • ✓Cast iron: old cast iron drains are rough inside and often rusted through. Strong acid and heat can thin them further.
  • ✓Clay and mixed joints: clay pipe with cement joints can crack, and chemicals sit in low spots where the pipe sags.
  • ✓Traps and fittings: a cleaner that cannot clear a full blockage just sits and softens plastic and seals.
  • ✓Tub and sink finishes: strong cleaners can etch porcelain and pit chrome.
  • ✓People: any chemical left in a clogged pipe is a splash risk when a plumber opens the trap.
Kitchen lines

Grease is the real kitchen clog

Kitchen drain clogs are mostly cooled grease with food bits stuck in it. Hot water washes it down the pipe, where it cools and sticks again further along. The fix is to stop pouring it.

Brookline's sewer overflow guidance tells residents to never pour grease down sink drains or into toilets, and notes that wipes do not degrade in the sewer system and can cause clogs. Wipe pans with a paper towel and put the towel in the trash, and keep cooking oil in a can.

When a kitchen line keeps clogging, a snake only opens a hole through the grease. A drain cleaning visit with a cable clears the full line, and heavy buildup may call for hydro jetting.

Know when to stop

When to quit the home remedy and call

Call us if water sits in a basin after two tries, if more than one fixture is slow, if a toilet bubbles when you run a sink, or if you smell sewage. Those are signs the blockage is deeper than a trap.

A backup into a basement floor drain or tub is urgent. Stop using water, keep people away from the wet area, and call dispatch. Do not reach for another bottle of chemical, and do not run a power auger in a pipe you cannot see.

Brookline note

Who owns which part of a clogged drain in Brookline

Brookline's DPW says the homeowner is responsible for the sewer service line from and including the connection to the public sewer main, and the Town is responsible for blockages within the public main. Brookline's Water and Sewer emergency line, 617-730-2175, takes backup reports around the clock. If neighbors on your street are backed up too, that call matters.

A sewer pipe must be repaired by a licensed drain layer, per the same DPW FAQ. Clearing a blocked drain does not need a permit under Massachusetts plumbing rules, but cutting into a pipe does, and homeowners cannot pull plumbing permits in Brookline.

The Census Bureau's 2020 to 2024 survey puts the median Brookline home at 1938, so cast iron and older materials are common in the building drains. That is why we do not recommend harsh chemistry here.

Know before you call

What affects the cost of clearing a stubborn drain

A drain that the fizz did not fix is a mechanical problem, and the price follows the work.

  • Fixture or main lineA single sink trap is quicker than a main drain that serves the whole house.
  • AccessA cleanout makes the job faster. No cleanout means working through a trap or from the roof vent.
  • Length and toolsA short snake run costs less than a long run or a jetter for heavy grease.
  • Camera inspectionLooking inside the pipe adds time but shows whether a collapse or roots caused the clog.
  • After-hours timingDispatch is open around the clock, and night calls can be priced differently.

Most policies do not pay for clearing a clog. Sewage damage from a backup may be covered only with a water backup endorsement.
