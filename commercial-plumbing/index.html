Urgent

When a business plumbing problem is an emergency

Call at once for sewage backing up from a floor drain, water spreading across a dining room or stockroom, no water at all, or a leak near a panel, walk-in cooler or point-of-sale equipment. Health inspectors, tenants below and neighbors on the other side of the wall all add pressure.

Close the main or the nearest fixture valve, switch off equipment that is standing in water only if you can reach the switch dry, and move stock off the floor. Then tell dispatch the type of business, the floor and what you see.

Storefronts

Retail and restaurant strips: Beacon, Harvard and Route 9

Many Brookline businesses sit in older multi-story buildings along Beacon Street, Harvard Street and Boylston Street (Route 9). Those buildings were built for other uses, so a restaurant may share a drain stack with apartments above, and a shop may drain through a basement line that has been patched many times.

That shared plumbing is why we trace a problem before we clear it. A backup that comes back may be a grease buildup in your line, or it may be a blockage in a shared stack that the building owner must also fix. We tell you which one it is.

Drain work

Floor drains, grease and the line beyond

Floor drains are the first place a blocked line shows itself. We clear them with a cable machine, camera the line when it will not stay clear, and use hydro jetting to scour grease from the pipe walls instead of boring a hole through it.

Grease is the common enemy in food service. The Town of Brookline's sewer overflow guidance says never to pour grease down sink drains, and it regulates non-stormwater discharges under Town By-Law 8.25. Where a restaurant has a grease interceptor, it needs cleaning on a schedule, and we point that out instead of repeatedly snaking a line that has no chance.

Water quality

Backflow, cross-connections and the Town program

Backflow means dirty water running backward into the clean supply. Brookline's Water and Sewer Division runs a cross-connection program under state rule 310 CMR 22.22, and 248 CMR lists protective devices by hazard in its cross-connection table.

Commercial equipment such as dishwashers, boilers, carbonators and irrigation lines is often where the hazard sits. If your building needs a device tested or added, we tell you what the plumbing code expects and send you to the Town's Water and Sewer business line at 617-730-2170 for the program side.

Honest limits

What we do not do

We service and repair plumbing in small and mid-sized commercial spaces. We do not design fire-suppression systems or large central plant piping, and we do not take on whole-building mechanical design.

Commercial leak tracing also has limits: pinpointing a hidden leak in a long run of pipe takes time, and results depend on access. Our process for that is covered on leak detection.

Know before you call

What sets the price of commercial work

A single clogged restroom drain and a grease-clogged trunk line are not the same visit. These factors drive the number.

  • Scope of the faultOne fixture, one drain, or a shared line each need different tools and time.
  • Access hoursWorking around service hours may mean early mornings or late nights, and we price that before starting.
  • Equipment neededCable machines, cameras and jetters add time and expense.
  • Building conditionsOld drain stacks, patched lines and shared risers take longer to trace.
  • Parts and code itemsNew backflow devices, valves or fixtures add parts and any required permit work.

Business property or tenant insurance may cover water damage from a sudden failure. Wear and neglected grease buildup usually are not covered. Check with your insurer and your landlord.
