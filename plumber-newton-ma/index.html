Housing and pipes

What the age of a Newton house tells us before we open a wall

We do not quote a year-built figure for Newton, because the city has not published one we can stand behind. The villages differ a lot. A house near an old village center can still carry original supply pipe, while a house on a newer street may already have been repiped.

The city itself has flagged the issue. Newton's own records review in 2016 looked at homes built before 1951 and identified 641 for lead-line inspection, and the city says many homes in Newton are known to have lead pipes and plumbing fixtures. The city also states that its water mains are not lead. So the risk sits on the private side, in the service line, old solder and older fixtures.

That is why an older Newton house gets a different first step from us. We trace what material enters the basement, check the main shut-off and look for corroded fittings before we recommend a repair. If the pipe is the problem, repiping is the honest answer, and we will say so.

Water and lead

Soft water, and why lead still matters here

MWRA supplies all of Newton's water, and MWRA's monthly report calls it soft, at about 1 grain per gallon across Metro Boston. Soft water means little scale on heaters, but it does not tell you what is inside your own pipes.

MWRA's 2023 community letter reported Newton's lead 90th percentile at 2.26 ppb against a 15 ppb action level. A sample result is not your tap, though. If your house is old, ask Newton DPW Utilities at 617-796-1640 about your service line before you assume anything.

Before we arrive

What to do in an older Newton house while you wait

Dispatch is open 24/7, and a few minutes of preparation protect your floors and speed up the visit.

Find the main shut-off

In many older houses it sits on the basement wall where the service line enters. Turn it clockwise until it stops, then open the lowest tap to drain the line.

Kill power near water

If a ceiling is dripping near a light fixture or outlet, turn off that breaker and keep everyone out of the wet area.

Stop using drains

Do not run the washer, dishwasher or showers during a backup. Every flush pushes more into the lowest fixture.

Smell gas? Leave first

If you smell gas near a heater or boiler, leave the building and call 911 or National Grid (1-800-233-5325) before calling us.
