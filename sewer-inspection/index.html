Sewer Inspection in Brookline, MA

A camera on a flexible rod travels down your sewer lateral and records what the pipe looks like. You get video, a plain explanation and a clear price for any follow-up work.

Call (617) 752-0573

A sewer inspection is a planned camera run through the line that carries waste from your building to the public sewer. Buyers order it before closing on an older house, and owners order it after backups that keep returning. Call dispatch, tell us the address and your timeline, and we schedule a visit, with same-day appointments when available.

The median Brookline home was built in 1938 per Census ACS estimates, so the pipe under many properties is older than anyone alive who installed it. A camera replaces guesswork with a recording you can show a seller, a lawyer or an insurer.

The process

How a sewer camera inspection works

The visit usually takes a fraction of a day. If the line is packed with debris, we clear it first so the lens can pass.

  1. Find the entry point. A cleanout near the basement floor or the foundation is best. Without one, we pull a toilet or use a roof vent.
  2. Run the camera. A self-leveling color camera on a push rod travels toward the street while we record video and footage.
  3. Locate the pipe. A transmitter in the camera head lets a handheld receiver mark the pipe's position and depth on the ground.
  4. Review together. We stop on any defect, show it on the screen and explain what it means for you.
  5. Deliver the video. You get the recording and a plain-language list of findings.

What it shows

What the camera can show you

  • ✓Pipe material. Clay, cast iron, plastic or fiber each look different on camera and age differently.
  • ✓Cracks and offsets. Broken sections and joints that have slid out of line.
  • ✓Roots. Where they enter and how dense the mass is.
  • ✓Bellies. Low spots that hold standing water and collect solids.
  • ✓Scale and grease. Buildup on the wall that narrows the opening.
  • ✓Connections. Branch tie-ins, a backwater valve, a cleanout and a missing section of pipe.
Honest limits

What a camera cannot tell you

The lens cannot see through standing water, so a belly full of water shows as a gap in the picture. It cannot pass a full collapse. It does not measure how fast a crack leaks, and it cannot say what the soil outside the pipe is doing.

It also cannot show the exact property line. The Town's FAQ places the homeowner's responsibility from and including the connection to the public main, so footage to the street helps, but the Town decides what is public. If your report finds nothing, that is a good result, and we say so.

Home buyers

Inspecting the lateral before you close

A standard home inspection often does not put a camera in the sewer. Because the lateral is the owner's responsibility, it becomes yours at closing, so many buyers add a camera visit during the inspection period. Ask the seller for access and a cleanout location, and book early because contract deadlines are short.

In older multi-family buildings the lateral often serves several units. Brookline has many two-family and larger buildings, so one lateral can carry the waste of several households. Knowing its shape before you buy is worth a call.

Recurring backups

When repeat clogs call for a camera

If you have paid for cabling more than once, a camera shows why. Roots, a belly or a broken joint each need a different fix. See sewer line cleaning for clearing the line and sewer line repair for fixing a failed pipe.

A camera is also useful after a repair to confirm the new section is straight and connected. We run the same inspection for buyers in nearby Newton, where lateral rules may differ, so ask the city before assuming.

Know before you call

What sets the price of a sewer inspection

We give a clear price before work starts. A few things shape it.

  • AccessAn existing cleanout is simple. Pulling a toilet or going through a vent takes longer.
  • Line length and layoutA short straight run is quicker than a long lateral with bends and branches.
  • DebrisIf the line needs cleaning before the camera can pass, that is added work.
  • LocatingMarking the pipe's path and depth on the ground adds time, and is worth having before digging.
  • ReportA written summary and the video for a closing or insurer is part of the visit.

Some homeowner policies cover sewer backup only with an endorsement. A dated video of the line can help document its condition, but check what your policy says before you rely on it.
