Signs of a failed tank

How to tell a dead tank from a tripped breaker

  • ✓Water pooling under the tank. If it seeps from the bottom seam or the jacket and not from a fitting or the relief valve pipe, the steel liner has rusted through.
  • ✓Rusty or brown hot water. Cold water that runs clear while the hot side runs brown points to corrosion inside the tank.
  • ✓Rumbling or popping at the burner. Sediment baked onto the bottom traps steam, and a tank full of it has usually lost capacity.
  • ✓A pilot or igniter that keeps failing. On a heater past 12 or 15 years, repeated lockouts often signal a worn-out burner assembly.

The decision

Repair or replace: where the line falls

Age and the location of the leak decide most cases. A fitting that drips can be tightened or swapped in an hour, while a leaking tank wall cannot be patched.

Repair or replace at a glance
What you seeUsual answerWhy
Leak from the tank bottom or seamReplaceThe liner has failed and water will keep escaping
Drip at a pipe fitting or drain valveRepairThe parts are small and the tank is intact

The visit

What a same-day tank swap involves

A standard tank replacement runs a few hours when the basement or closet is easy to reach. Tight stairs or a heater wedged behind a furnace slow the carry-out, so tell dispatch about access when you call.

  1. Shut down and drain. We close the water and fuel, then drain the old tank to a floor drain or through a hose to a sink.
  2. Disconnect and remove. Supply lines, gas line or electric whip, and the flue are separated and the old tank goes out.
  3. Set and connect the new heater. New flexible connectors, a cold-side shut-off valve and a temperature and pressure relief valve go on, with the discharge pipe run full size and ending within twelve inches of the floor per 248 CMR.
  4. Fill, purge and test. We fill the tank, bleed air at a hot tap, light or power it up and check the flue draft on a gas unit.
  5. Permit. Replacing a heater needs a plumbing permit. Homeowners cannot pull one in Brookline, so your plumber pulls it.
Worth knowing

Expansion tanks and check valves

A check valve is not allowed on the cold supply to a water heater under 248 CMR. If a meter or backflow device has made the house a closed system, the code calls for a properly sized thermal expansion tank, and we check for one on every swap.

Heat pump option

Heat pump water heaters and Mass Save

A heat pump water heater moves heat from the surrounding air instead of making it with a coil or a burner, so it uses far less electricity than a standard electric tank. Mass Save lists a rebate for heat pump water heaters that replace electric, propane, gas or oil units, and you should check Mass Save for current terms before you buy.

It also needs floor space, a condensate drain and open air around it, so a cramped basement closet may rule it out. Ask when you call.

Before we arrive

Leaking tank or no hot water right now: what to do first

Most of the damage from a failed tank comes from the hour after it starts leaking. These steps are safe for anyone to do.

Close the cold supply

Turn the valve on the pipe entering the top of the heater clockwise. If it will not move, shut the main valve where the water line enters the building.

Cut the fuel or power

Turn a gas control knob to off or switch off the heater's breaker. Never reach for the breaker if you are standing in water.

Smell gas? Leave

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or National Grid at 1-800-233-5325 before calling us.

Move what is on the floor

Lift boxes, rugs and stored items out of the path of the water, and open a floor drain if you have one.

Brookline's water comes from MWRA, and MWRA's monthly report describes it as soft, about 1 grain per gallon. Soft water leaves less scale in a tank, so heaters here tend to fail from corrosion and age more than from mineral buildup.

Know before you call

What moves the price of a new water heater

  • Fuel and typeGas, electric, standard tank and heat pump units each carry different equipment and installation costs.
  • Tank sizeA 40 gallon tank and an 80 gallon tank are different pieces of equipment, and the right size depends on how many people shower each morning.
  • Venting and gas line conditionA flue that needs new pipe, or a gas connector that cannot be reused, adds parts and time.
  • Access and haul-awayBasement stairs, tight closets and removal of the old tank change the labor.
  • Code itemsAn expansion tank, a new relief valve and discharge pipe, and the permit are part of doing the job to code.

Some homeowner and landlord policies cover sudden water damage from a failed tank but not the tank itself. Ask your insurer before the old unit leaves the building, and keep photos of the leak.
