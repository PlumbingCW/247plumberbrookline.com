Emergency Sewer Line Cleaning in Brookline, MA

When every drain in the building is slow and the basement floor drain is filling, the clog is in the main sewer line. We clear the pipe you own and help you work out whether the blockage sits on your side of the property line.

Call (617) 752-0573

A sewer backup is urgent, and our dispatch answers 24/7. Stop using every fixture in the building right now, including toilets, laundry and dishwashers. Keep children and pets out of the wet area, stay away from outlets and the furnace if water is near them, and do not use a wet vacuum on sewage.

Sewer line cleaning clears the main drain that carries all of the building's waste out to the public sewer. It is different from clearing a sink, because the clog is typically 30 to 80 feet from the fixture and lives in a pipe that is 4 inches or larger. We use a large cable machine with cutting heads, a camera, and hydro jetting when the buildup calls for it.

Before we arrive

Sewer backup: what to do first

The next ten minutes decide how much damage the water does.

Stop adding flow

Every flush or load of laundry adds gallons to a line that is already full. Ask upstairs neighbors to stop too.

Ask the street

Check whether a neighbor has the same problem. If several buildings back up at once, the Town may need to look at the public main.

Photograph the damage

Take pictures of the water line, wet items and any visible debris. An insurer will ask for them later.

Open a path

Clear belongings away from the basement cleanout so the machine can reach the line.

Who owns what

Your side or the Town's: the Brookline split

The Town of Brookline's water and sewer FAQ states that the homeowner is responsible for the service line from and including the connection to the public sewer main. The Town is responsible for blockages within the public sewer main. A repair to the sewer pipe must be done by a licensed drain layer.

If only your building is backed up, the clog is probably in the lateral. If the whole block is affected, call the Town's 24/7 water and sewer emergency line at 617-730-2175 and then call us for your side. In Boston neighborhoods such as Brighton, the Boston Water and Sewer Commission handles the street main, and the owner is responsible for the whole lateral.

What we find

Why a building lateral clogs in the first place

Census ACS estimates for 2020 to 2024 give 1938 as the median year Brookline homes were built, so many laterals are decades old. Older pipe often has rough walls, offset joints or low spots where solids settle, and every one of those catches paper and grease.

Roots are the other usual cause. They creep into a joint searching for moisture, then grow into a mat across the bore. Wipes sold as flushable also gather in the same places, and the Town's sewer guidance warns they do not break down in the sewer.

Symptoms

Signs the main sewer line is the problem

  • ✓Lowest drain fills first. A basement shower or floor drain bubbles or overflows when anyone upstairs flushes.
  • ✓Toilet and tub together. Flushing a toilet makes the tub gurgle or rise.
  • ✓Sewer odor outside. A sour smell near the cleanout or a wet patch in the yard.
  • ✓More than one fixture. Several drains slow at once rather than just one sink.
Backwater valve

Backwater valves: what the Town's policy says

A backwater valve is a check device in the building drain that keeps sewage from flowing backward into low fixtures when the street main surcharges. Brookline's Backwater Valve Policy covers fixtures located below the roadway. It cites 248 CMR 10.15, requires a licensed plumber and a Building Department permit, and puts installation and maintenance on the owner.

That last point matters. A valve that is never opened and cleaned can jam shut and cause a backup of its own, so we check it during a cleaning visit. Homeowners cannot pull plumbing permits in Brookline, so the licensed plumber pulls it.
