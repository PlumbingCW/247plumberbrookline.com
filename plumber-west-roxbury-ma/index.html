What the neighborhood calls for

The jobs a West Roxbury single-family house tends to need

Boston's own planning profile counts about 70 percent of West Roxbury's properties as single-family houses, and most of those are owner-occupied. A house on its own lot has its own sewer lateral, its own water service line, its own water heater and often its own boiler. Each one is a separate failure point, and each one is yours to deal with.

  • ✓Sewer backup. Gurgling toilets, water rising in a basement floor drain or a tub that fills when the washer drains. This is usually a lateral problem, so start with sewer line cleaning.
  • ✓No hot water. A tank that has quit, is leaking at the base or is rumbling loudly. Sometimes it is a repair, sometimes the tank is done, and we say which. See water heater replacement if it is the second.
  • ✓Heat that will not fire. A boiler that locks out on a cold night needs a technician the same day. Our boiler repair page covers what we check.
  • ✓Water in the basement. A sump pump that hums but does not move water, or one that never kicks on. Details are on the sump pump repair page.
  • ✓A clog that will not clear. Kitchen lines packed with grease and tub drains packed with hair are the common ones, and we handle both with drain cleaning.

Who owns what

When the backup is yours and when it is the street's

Boston Water and Sewer Commission, or BWSC, maintains the sewer main in the street. According to BWSC, the property owner is responsible for the entire lateral, meaning the pipe from your house out to that main. That one rule decides who pays for most backups.

Housing and water

What the housing mix means for your plumbing

The same city profile puts two-family homes at about 10 percent of West Roxbury properties and condos at about 19 percent. A condo owner should ask the association whether a stack or a shared drain line is involved before paying for a repair that is really the building's. Single-family owners have no such question: the lateral and the service line are theirs.

Water here comes from MWRA reservoirs through BWSC, and MWRA describes its water as soft, about 1 grain per gallon of hardness. Soft water builds less scale inside a tank heater than hard water does, but sediment still collects, and a tank that is not drained and checked will eventually fail. We do not guess a heater's age from the neighborhood. We read the data plate.

Before we arrive

What to do in the first few minutes

A few quick steps keep a bad hour from turning into a bad week.

Find the main shut-off

Look for the valve where the water line enters the house, often near the meter. Turn it clockwise to stop a spraying or running leak.

Stop using the drains

If the basement floor drain is rising, do not run the washer or flush. Every gallon you add goes to the lowest opening.

Cut power near water

If water reaches the furnace, water heater or panel, do not stand in it. Leave the breaker alone if you cannot reach it dry and say so when you call.

Smell gas? Leave

If you smell gas, get out and call 911 or National Grid at 1-800-233-5325 before calling us.

What moves the cost of a West Roxbury call

We give a clear price before work starts, and the number depends on what we find. A small clog and a failed lateral are very different jobs.

  • Where the problem isA trap under a sink is a short visit. A lateral running under a front yard or driveway is a longer one.
  • AccessA clean-out near the foundation lets us get a cable or camera in fast. Without one, we may have to go in through a toilet or roof vent.
  • Pipe conditionRoots, offsets and collapsed sections change a clearing into a repair.
  • Hour and urgencyA same-day or after-hours emergency is scheduled differently from a planned visit, and we tell you the price before the work starts.
  • PermitsWork that needs a plumbing permit goes through Boston's Inspectional Services Department, and your plumber pulls it.
