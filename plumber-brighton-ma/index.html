Common calls

The problems Brighton buildings call about

Most of our Brighton work starts in a building with more than one household under the same roof. The symptom shows up in one unit, but the cause is often in another, or in a shared pipe behind a wall.

  • ✓A wet or stained ceiling. Water comes through the plaster below a bathroom or kitchen, and nobody upstairs sees a leak.
  • ✓A floor drain that backs up. Water rises in the basement drain when a laundry machine runs or the toilets above are flushed.
  • ✓No hot water. A tank leaks onto the basement slab, or the pilot and burner will not stay lit.
  • ✓Cold radiators in heating season. Steam or hot-water heat dies on a cold night and several apartments lose it together.
  • ✓A water bill that jumped. The meter keeps turning when every tap is off, which usually means a running toilet or a hidden leak.

Leaks you cannot see

Finding the source of a ceiling leak in a multi-unit building

A stain on a first-floor ceiling can come from a tub drain, a supply line, a radiator valve or a roof. Guessing wastes a day and opens the wrong wall. We trace it in order, starting with the fixtures directly above the stain.

Our crews use a moisture meter and thermal camera to map the wet area. For a hidden supply leak, a pressure test or leak detection with acoustic listening gear narrows it to a few feet before we cut any plaster.

Basement backups

When the basement drain backs up, who owns the pipe?

Boston Water and Sewer Commission (BWSC) maintains the sewer main in the street. The property owner is responsible for the entire lateral, the pipe that runs from the building to that main. That split decides who pays when the line fails.

If several drains back up together, the blockage is usually in the building's main drain or the lateral. We clear it with a cable machine or hydro jetting, then camera the line to see whether roots, grease or a broken joint caused it. A camera pass is how we tell a simple clog from a pipe that needs sewer line repair.

BWSC runs a 24-hour emergency line at 617-989-7000 for main breaks and street-side sewer problems. BWSC also offers a grant program for failed laterals, so ask them for current terms before you commit to a repair.

Hot water and heat

No hot water or no heat: what to check before you call

A leaking tank cannot be patched. If water pools under a gas or electric heater, shut the cold supply and power it down, then call. Our water heater repair visits cover thermostats, elements, burners and relief valves, and we replace the tank when the leak is at the shell.

State heating rules require 68 degrees in habitable rooms from 7 a.m. to 11 p.m. and 64 degrees overnight, from September 15 through June 15. For steam or hot-water systems, our boiler repair technicians check the low-water cutoff, circulator and zone valves. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or National Grid at 1-800-233-5325 before calling us.

Housing context

Why Brighton's mix of condos, rentals and two-families matters for plumbing

Boston's planning profile for Allston and Brighton, using 2010 figures, counted about 7,800 condos and roughly 2,500 two-family buildings. About one in five units was owner-occupied. Most of the rest were rentals.

That mix means shared risers, shared drains and a landlord or condo association behind many repairs. We write up what we find in plain terms so an owner, a manager or a tenant can each see what needs to happen. If a shared stack is failing, repiping is an honest option we will quote.

Water and sewer

How water reaches Brighton and who to call about the street

Brighton's water comes through BWSC from MWRA's reservoirs, and MWRA reports it as soft, at about 1 grain per gallon. Soft water means scale is rarely the cause of a failing fixture here. Pipe age, pressure and wear usually are.

BWSC replaces private lead service lines free for eligible owners and runs a lead hotline at (617) 989-7888. If your building's water line is old or leaking at the meter, call BWSC first for the street side, and call us for water service line repair on the owner's side.

Before we arrive

What to do in a Brighton building while you wait

These steps protect the unit below you and make our first hour faster.

Find your valve

Look for the main shut-off in the basement or at the meter, and for the fixture stops under sinks and toilets. In a multi-unit building, ask the owner or manager which valve feeds your unit.

Protect the floor below

Place buckets under ceiling drips, move furniture off wet carpet and pull electronics away from the stain.

Keep the drains quiet

If the basement drain is backing up, stop laundry and flushing in every unit until we clear the line.

Tell us the floor

Tell dispatch which floor is wet, which units are affected and whether the heater or boiler is running.

What moves the price of a Brighton plumbing repair

We give you a clear price before work starts. These are the factors that change it.

  • AccessA pipe behind a bathroom wall in a third-floor unit takes longer to reach than a drain in an open basement.
  • Building layoutA shared riser or stack that serves several units can mean coordination with the owner or association.
  • Pipe materialOlder steel or cast iron may need more cutting and fittings than copper or PVC.
  • Time of the callLate-night and weekend dispatch involves different staffing than a daytime visit.
