Symptoms

What your toilet is telling you

Some toilet problems are a ten-minute fix and some are not. These are the patterns we see most in Brookline bathrooms.

  • ✓Phantom refills. The tank hisses or tops up every few minutes with nobody home. Water is slipping past the flapper into the bowl.
  • ✓Constant running. The fill valve never shuts off and water trickles down the overflow tube in the middle of the tank.
  • ✓Weak or half flush. The rim jets are partly blocked, or the flush valve drops closed too early.
  • ✓Gurgling after a flush. Air is being pulled through the trap, often by a partial clog past the toilet. That side of the problem is drain cleaning work.
  • ✓Rocking, or a damp ring at the base. The wax seal has failed or the flange underneath is damaged.

Inside the tank

A running toilet: four parts, four different fixes

Start with a dye test. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank, wait 15 minutes without flushing, and look at the bowl. Color in the bowl means the flapper or its seat is leaking.

If the water stays clear but the tank keeps refilling, look at the fill valve and the float height instead.

Common tank parts and what failure looks like
PartFailure signUsual fix
FlapperDye reaches the bowl, phantom refillsNew rubber flapper, clean the valve seat
Fill valveHissing, tank overfills, water never stopsNew fill valve, reset the float height
Flush valveCracked seat or a leaking towerNew flush valve and tank-to-bowl gasket
Supply line and stopDrip at the wall or under the tankNew 3/8 inch braided line or a new stop valve

Rubber and plastic parts wear with use. Mineral scale is a less common culprit here, because MWRA reports its water as soft, about 1 grain per gallon.

At the floor

Leaking at the base: wax ring, closet bolts and flange

Water around the base is not always the bowl. It can be tank sweat, a loose supply nut, or a failed wax seal between the bowl and the closet flange. We dry everything, rock-test the bowl, and check both closet bolts for play.

A rocking toilet crushes its wax ring over time. If the flange is cracked, corroded, or sits below the finished floor, the repair is a flange ring or a new flange, then a fresh wax ring and new brass closet bolts. Tightening the old bolts on a damaged flange only hides the leak and can crack the porcelain.

Soft or stained subfloor around the base tells us water has been escaping for a while. We show you that before we set the new seal, not after.

Urgent

When a toilet overflow is an emergency

A bowl that rises instead of draining is the one to treat as urgent. Stop flushing and close the supply stop. If other fixtures gurgle or the tub shows water, stay off every drain in the unit, because that points to a blockage past the toilet.

Clear water from a stuck fill valve ends when the stop closes. Sewage does not, so keep children and pets away, open a window, and call. We clear the bowl with a closet auger, and if the line itself is blocked we move to sewer line cleaning.

Before we arrive

Older buildings

When the shutoff valve will not shut off

The median Brookline home was built in 1938, and about half of all units date to 1939 or earlier, per Census ACS data. Many bathrooms in those buildings still have decades-old stops that seize or weep the moment you turn them.

If yours will not close, shut the unit or building main and call. We replace the stop with a new quarter-turn valve and a braided supply line during the visit. The same failure happens under sinks, which is why faucet repair visits often start with the stops.

Water off to one toilet only usually means a closed stop, a clogged screen in the fill valve, or a kinked line. It is rarely a street problem.

Know before you call

What moves the price of a toilet job

A flapper swap and a flange rebuild with a new bowl are very different jobs. These are the factors that set the number.

  • Parts or full replacementTank parts are a short visit. A new toilet adds the bowl, the seal, the bolts and disposal of the old fixture.
  • Flange and floor conditionA cracked flange or soft subfloor adds repair work before the new seal can go on.
  • Access and shutoffsA dead stop valve means replacing it first, which adds parts and time.
  • Fixture choiceComfort height, elongated bowls and wall-hung models change both the product and the labor.
  • Time of the callA late-night overflow is dispatched the same way as a daytime one, and the after-hours part of the visit is quoted before we start.

If water reached the ceiling below, your homeowner's or renter's policy may respond to the damage. Ask your insurer before cleanup finishes. A burst supply line is more likely to be covered than slow wear.
