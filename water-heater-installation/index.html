Water Heater Installation in Brookline, MA

A planned install is the easiest time to fix the things an emergency swap skips: venting that no longer fits the house, a missing expansion tank, a heater in the wrong room.

Call (617) 752-0573

Water heater installation in Brookline, MA covers three kinds of jobs: replacing a working but aging tank before it fails, switching fuel or type, and relocating the heater to a better spot. We schedule these visits ahead, with same-day appointments when available if your plans move up.

If your tank is already leaking, skip ahead to our water heater replacement page, which is built for the urgent version of this job. This page is for people with time to choose the unit, the location and the venting.

Planning the job

What we settle before the heater arrives

A good install starts with five answers. Getting them before the truck rolls keeps the day short and the surprises small.

  • Fuel. . Gas, electric or a heat pump unit, and whether you want to keep the fuel you have.
  • Size. . Tank capacity matched to the number of bathrooms and people, not to what the old label said.
  • Location. . Floor space, clearance, a drain or pan, and a route for the relief discharge pipe.
  • Venting. . Chimney liner, direct vent through a side wall, or none for electric and most heat pump units.
  • Access. . Stairs, doors and who holds the key in a shared building.

Venting

Venting and fuel: the part that changes the price

An older gas heater that vents into a masonry chimney can lose its draft once a new high-efficiency furnace or boiler stops sharing that flue. A tank that backdrafts spills exhaust into the basement, so we check the flue draft with the unit running, and where the chimney is oversized or unlined we talk through a direct-vent model that exits through the side wall.

Electric and heat pump heaters need no flue at all, which opens up locations a gas unit could never use. If you smell gas at any point, leave the building and call 911 or National Grid at 1-800-233-5325 before calling us.

Code

Expansion tanks, relief valves and check valves

248 CMR 10.00 gives plain rules we follow on every install. A check valve may not sit on the cold supply to the heater. If a water meter with a check valve or a backflow preventer makes the system closed, a properly sized thermal expansion tank is required.

The temperature and pressure relief valve discharges through a full-size pipe with no shut-off, ending within twelve inches of the floor. The code table lists 120 degrees Fahrenheit as the minimum and 130 as the maximum for residential hot water, and we set the new heater inside that range.

Permits

Permits and inspection in Brookline

Under state law, plumbing permits are issued only to licensed plumbers. Brookline does not let homeowners pull plumbing or gas permits, so we file the paperwork through the town's Accela portal, and review takes about 3 days.

This job needs a permit and an inspection by the town. If you are in a condo or rental, ask the building owner whether the association has its own rules about heater work before you set a date.

Install day

How the install day runs

  1. Walk-through. A technician confirms the plan, the shut-offs and the discharge pipe path.
  2. Drain and disconnect. The old unit is shut down, drained and disconnected from water, fuel and venting.
  3. Set and connect. The new heater goes in with fresh connectors, a shut-off valve, relief valve and expansion tank where needed.
  4. Start-up. Air is purged from the lines, the burner or elements are brought on and the flue draft is checked.
  5. Walk-through again. We show you the shut-offs, the temperature setting and the drain valve for future flushing.
Brookline homes

Moving a heater in an older Brookline building

Relocating a heater sounds simple until you see the pipe runs. Brookline's median home was built in 1938, and in a house that age the old location is often tied to a chimney. A move can mean new venting, or switching to an electric or heat pump unit that needs none.

Brookline's water is soft, about 1 grain per gallon according to MWRA, which means less scale inside a new tank. We also install in nearby Jamaica Plain and other neighboring areas, including similar older housing.

Know before you call

What moves the price of an install

  • Type and fuelA heat pump unit, a condensing tankless and a basic gas tank are different purchases.
  • Venting workNew flue pipe, a liner or a side-wall vent adds materials and labor.
  • RelocationMoving the heater means new supply, drain and discharge piping.
  • Code itemsAn expansion tank, a relief valve and discharge pipe, and the permit.
  • Access and haul-awayNarrow stairs and removal of the old tank take time.

A planned install is not a covered loss on a homeowner policy. If a Mass Save rebate applies to your unit, it can help, so check Mass Save for current terms.
