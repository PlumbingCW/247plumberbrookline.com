Tankless Water Heater in Brookline, MA

A tankless unit makes hot water as you use it, so it does not run out mid-shower. It also asks more of your venting and gas line than a tank does, and in an older building those two items decide whether it fits.

A tankless water heater in Brookline, MA is often a good answer for a household that empties a 40 gallon tank every morning, and a poor answer for a one-person condo with a tight utility closet. We install, repair and flush condensing tankless units, and we tell you plainly when a tank is the smarter buy.

How it works

What a condensing tankless unit does differently

A condensing model pulls extra heat out of the exhaust by running it past a second heat exchanger. The cooled exhaust turns to acidic water called condensate, so the unit needs a drain and a plastic vent pipe instead of a metal flue.

Because the exhaust is cool, the vent can run horizontally out a side wall in plastic pipe. In a Brookline basement with a retired chimney, that opens a path a conventional gas tank could not use.

Fit check

Signs a tankless heater suits your home

  • ✓A side wall or short path outside. The vent and a fresh air intake need an exterior route.
  • ✓A gas line that can carry the load. Tankless burners draw far more gas at once than a tank burner, so a small existing line often needs upsizing.
  • ✓A floor drain or condensate route. The acidic condensate must reach a drain.
  • ✓High hot water demand. Two showers and a dishwasher at once strain a tank but not a correctly sized tankless.
  • ✓Wall space. Most units hang on a wall, which frees floor space in a cramped basement.
When to skip it

When a tankless heater does not make sense

Tankless units cost more up front, and savings take years to show. A small household that uses little hot water may never recoup the difference.

A condo with no exterior wall access, or an association that bans new penetrations, can rule it out outright. So can a gas meter or line that cannot be upsized, and low-flow fixtures that fall below the unit's minimum flow so it never fires. In those cases a tank, or a heat pump model, is the better choice.

Maintenance

Flushing and descaling a tankless heater

Scale builds on the heat exchanger and shrinks its water passages, so units need periodic flushing with a pump and a descaling solution. Brookline's MWRA water is soft, about 1 grain per gallon, so scale builds more slowly here than in hard-water towns, but it does not disappear.

A yearly flush is still sensible, and skipping it can cost you efficiency and trigger error codes. If your unit beeps and shows a fault code, note the code before you call, since it tells the technician which sensor or vent to test first.

Code and permits

What the town and the code expect

Gas heater work requires a permit, and Brookline does not let homeowners pull plumbing or gas permits. Permit review is about 3 days, and the plumber files for you.

248 CMR also requires a properly sized thermal expansion tank where a meter or backflow device has closed the system, and a relief valve discharge ending within twelve inches of the floor. If you smell gas, leave and call 911 or National Grid at 1-800-233-5325 before calling us.

Know before you call

What sets the price of a tankless install

The unit is only part of the number. These factors move it most.

  • Gas line upsizingThe distance from the meter and the existing pipe size decide how much new pipe is needed.
  • Venting routeA short run out a side wall costs less than a long run through floors.
  • Condensate drainHow far the nearest drain sits changes the piping.
  • Unit capacityA larger household needs a higher-output unit.
  • Removal and permitTaking out the old tank and pulling the permit are part of the job.

A planned upgrade is not a covered loss. Mass Save rebates for water heating have focused on heat pump units, so check Mass Save before assuming one applies to a gas tankless.
