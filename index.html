While you wait

What to do in the first five minutes of a plumbing emergency

The steps below limit damage before a plumber arrives. None of them needs tools, and all of them are safe to do yourself.

  1. Shut off the water. Turn the main valve clockwise, usually near the meter in the basement. In a condo or apartment, look for the unit shutoff under a sink or in a utility closet.
  2. Stop using drains. If a drain is backing up, do not flush or run the dishwasher or washing machine, because that water has nowhere to go.
  3. Keep power and water apart. Stay out of a flooded basement if water is near outlets, a panel or a plugged-in appliance. Call 911 if you cannot do so safely.
  4. Smell gas? Leave first. Get everyone outside and call 911 or National Grid at 1-800-233-5325 before you call us.
  5. Call dispatch. Phone (617) 752-0573 and tell us what you see. We ask a few quick questions and send the right crew.

Water and sewer

What the town and MWRA handle, and what is yours

Brookline's water comes from MWRA's Quabbin and Wachusett reservoirs, and MWRA reports it as soft, around one grain per gallon. That means little scale in tanks and fixtures, so most water heater trouble here comes from age and pressure rather than mineral buildup.

The line from the sidewalk shutoff to the building is the homeowner's responsibility, and so is the sewer service line from the building to the public main. The Town handles blockages in the main itself. If you are unsure which side a problem is on, call the Town's 24/7 water and sewer emergency line at 617-730-2175, then call us for the part that is yours.

Brookline reports no identified lead service lines as of the end of 2025, and the Town replaces any private-side lead line it finds at no cost to the customer. Plumbing inside the building is a separate question, and in a pre-1940 house it is worth a look.
