Urgent

When a sump pump failure is an emergency

If the basin is full, the pump hums but does not move water, or the floor has started to fill, call now. A pump that sits idle during a heavy rain gives groundwater time to reach the slab.

Check three simple things before you call: the plug and GFCI outlet, the float, and the discharge pipe outside. A tripped outlet or a stuck float is easy to spot and quick to tell dispatch about.

Symptoms

Signs the pump is failing

  • ✓It runs but the water does not drop. A worn impeller, a clogged intake or a failed check valve lets the water fall back.
  • ✓It never turns on. A stuck float, a dead switch or a burned-out motor.
  • ✓It cycles constantly. The check valve is leaking back or the float is set too low.
  • ✓It is loud, rattling or grinding. Debris or bearing wear.
  • ✓Power out during a storm and water rising. A battery backup is the fix. See the section below.

Storm risk

Why backup power matters near the Muddy River

The National Weather Service recorded the Muddy River at Brookline cresting at 15.41 feet on March 15, 2010, above the 15.0 foot flood stage, in the mid-March 2010 floods. That event ended in a minor-category flood at the gauge.

We do not claim damage figures for that storm. The practical lesson is that heavy rain and high groundwater arrive together with power cuts. A battery backup with its own float keeps the basin clear when the main pump cannot run.

Discharge rules

Where the discharge goes

Pump water must go outside, away from the foundation. Brookline's sewer guidance states that downspouts and sump pumps do not discharge to sewer drains. Ask the Building Department where your discharge may go.

A check valve sits in the discharge line right above the pump so water does not run back into the basin. A weep hole drilled in the pipe below it relieves air lock, and a frost-proof outlet keeps the pipe from freezing in January.

Ejector pumps

Sewage ejectors and backwater valves

A basement bathroom or laundry below the sewer line often drains to a sewage ejector, not a sump. 248 CMR 10.15(9) says the discharge piping from all sewage pumps and ejectors shall contain an accessible backwater or check valve and an accessible full port shut off valve. For a single-family ejector pump, the same section sets a minimum of 20 gallons per minute.

A backwater valve is a different device. 248 CMR 10.15(10) says backwater valves may be installed in drain piping that receives the discharge only from fixtures or drains subject to backflow from the public sewer system. The Town's policy covers fixtures below the roadway, and requires a licensed plumber and a Building Department permit. The owner maintains the valve.

If an ejector failure is also a backup from the street line, see sewer line cleaning. For the wider basement question, read about basement waterproofing.

Know before you call

What moves the price of sump pump work

A float swap and a new pump with battery backup and discharge piping are different jobs. These factors set the number.

  • Repair or replacementA switch or check valve is a short visit. A new pump adds the unit, the piping and the install.
  • Backup systemA battery or water-powered backup adds a second unit, controls and a charger.
  • Basin and discharge conditionA cracked basin or a frozen, undersized discharge pipe adds work.
  • Ejector requirementsCheck valve, full port shutoff and venting needed by code add parts and labor.
  • TimingA storm-night dispatch uses the same line as a daytime call. Any after-hours amount is stated first.

Many homeowner policies exclude groundwater or sewer backup unless a rider was added. Ask your insurer what yours covers before you file.
